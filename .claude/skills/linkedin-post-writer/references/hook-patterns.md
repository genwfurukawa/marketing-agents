# Hook Patterns Reference
# Used by: linkedin-post-writer
# Purpose: Proven hook structures with worked examples for B2B SaaS AEO content

---

## The Quality Gate for Every Hook

Remove the founder's name from the hook.
Ask: could any marketing person have written this exact sentence?

If YES → too generic. Apply specificity test below.
If NO → proceed.

**Specificity test:**
- Does it name a number? ("7 of 10" not "most")
- Does it name a context? ("in the last 30 days" not "recently")
- Does it name a behavior? ("scored a 0 on comparison queries" not "invisible")
- Does it name a platform? ("in Perplexity" not "in AI search")

The more of these it answers, the more specific and citable it is.

## Hook Type A: Specific Observation

Use when the insight comes from direct work — audits, client results,
weekly tracking data.

**Structure:**
`[Number] of [context] [specific finding].`

**Templates:**
```
[N] of the [last/past] [N] [companies/clients/founders] [specific finding].
In [N] [weeks/months], [specific pattern observed].
[N]% of [companies in context] [specific behavior].
```

**Worked examples:**
```
7 of the last 10 companies I audited scored a 0 on comparison queries.

In 3 months of tracking, only 1 of 8 clients appeared in ChatGPT for
their top comparison query before we started working together.

Every RevOps SaaS I audited this quarter had the same gap: invisible
on comparison queries, visible on brand queries. Backwards from where
pipeline actually forms.
```

**Do NOT write:**
```
Most companies don't show up in AI search.  [too vague]
Many SaaS founders are missing this.  [no specificity]
I've noticed that AI visibility is becoming important.  [generic observation]
```

## Hook Type B: Surprising Data

Use when the insight contains a counterintuitive statistic or finding
that challenges what people assume.

**Structure:**
`[Stat or finding] — [brief why it matters or why it surprises].`

**Templates:**
```
[Specific finding with number]. [One-line context for why it surprises.]
[Number]% [specific finding]. [What people assumed instead.]
[Counterintuitive outcome]. Here's [what actually happened / why].
```

**Worked examples:**
```
Your top-ranking Google page probably doesn't get cited in ChatGPT.
The citation signals are completely different.

Only 15% of Google AI Overview sources overlap with traditional top-10 results.
Pages outside page 1 get cited when their structure is right.

Publishing 4 posts a week for a year. Zero appearances in Perplexity's
comparison answers. One restructured page changed that in 6 weeks.
```

**Research to cite when relevant:**
- Princeton GEO study (KDD 2024): statistics boost AI citation rate +37%
- Princeton GEO study: keyword stuffing reduces AI citation rate -10%
- SE Ranking analysis: domain authority accounts for ~40% of ChatGPT citation
- ZipTie analysis: content-answer fit accounts for ~55% of ChatGPT citation

## Hook Type C: Contrarian Claim

Use when the insight challenges a commonly held belief in the space.
Strongest when the POV library has a clear contrarian position.

**Structure:**
`[Common belief]. [Direct contradiction or reframe].`

**Templates:**
```
[What everyone says]. [What's actually true].
[Conventional advice]. That's the wrong move.
[Widely held assumption]. The data says otherwise.
```

**Worked examples:**
```
Publishing more content is making your AI visibility problem worse,
not better.

SEO gets you ranked. AEO gets you on the shortlist before buyers
even search Google.

Your buyers have already decided who to call. The decision happened
in ChatGPT, before your SDR sent a single message.

Founder-led content outperforms polished brand content in AI citations.
Specificity beats production quality.
```

**Warning:** Contrarian hooks need evidence. If the claim is contrarian
but the insight section has no data, the post reads as opinion-as-fact.
Only use this hook type if the evidence is strong.

## Hook Type D: Story Moment

Use when the insight came from a specific interaction — a call, a
conversation, a specific client situation.

**Structure:**
`[Person type] [specific thing they said or did] [time reference].`

**Templates:**
```
A [job title] told me [specific thing] [this week/this month].
[Number] [people/founders/CEOs] asked me [specific question] this month.
[Person type] sent me [specific thing] [time]. [One-line description of what it showed].
```

**Worked examples:**
```
A Series A CEO sent me their content calendar last week.
47 posts scheduled for Q1. Zero of them structured for AI citation.

Three CEOs in March didn't know the difference between AEO and GEO.
Neither did their marketing teams.

A Head of Marketing showed me their Perplexity rankings last month.
Their competitors were in 8 of 10 comparison answers. They were in 0.
```

**Key rule for Story Moment hooks:**
The story must be real. Do not fabricate a story moment.
If the insight didn't come from a specific interaction, use a different hook type.

## Hooks to Never Use

These patterns consistently underperform and are excluded from the system:

```
"I want to share something..." [announces intent instead of delivering value]
"Hot take:" [signals opinion without evidence]
"Unpopular opinion:" [same problem]
"Here's what I learned about..." [indirect, buries the insight]
"Quick thought:" [diminishes the value]
"[Rhetorical question]?" [questions don't create pattern interrupt]
"In today's..." [sets up generic thought leadership]
"We all know that..." [condescending framing]
"The future of [category] is..." [speculative, uncitable]
```

## Benchmark Performance Data

Posts that consistently generate profile visits and pipeline DMs share:
1. A specific number in the first 2 lines
2. An insight that can be screenshotted standalone
3. An implication that makes the reader think "is this happening to me?"

Posts that consistently underperform:
1. Generic advice without evidence ("you should optimize for AI")
2. Opinions presented as facts without data
3. Long context sections that delay the actual insight
