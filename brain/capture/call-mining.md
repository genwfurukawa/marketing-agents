# Mining sales calls

The highest value signal in most companies, and the least wired in. Recordings pile
up and nobody reads them, so the objections stay in the sales team's heads and never
reach the marketing.

## What to pull

| Looking for | Sounds like | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Objections | "How is this different from", "we already have" | `CONTEXT.md` |
| Buyer language | How they describe the problem before you reframe it | `BRAND.md` |
| Triggers | "We started looking because" | `ICP.md` |
| Disqualifiers | The deals that went quiet, and the shared pattern | `ICP.md` |
| Proof gaps | The claim that made them hesitate | `CONTEXT.md` |

## The method

Work in batches of ten calls: five won, five lost. Fewer than that and you are
reading anecdotes. Lost calls carry more information than won ones and get read less.

For each call, write one line: what they wanted, where it stalled, what they said in
their own words. Then look across the ten for what repeats. A thing said once is a
story. A thing said in six of ten calls is a truth, and truths change the Brain.

## The judgment that stays human

A recurring objection might be a blog post, or it might mean the positioning is
wrong. That call is not an agent's to make. Route topics to a loop. Route truths to
the Brain and tell a person.

## The trap

Do not let the sales team's summary stand in for the call. The summary is already an
interpretation, and the value is in the buyer's exact words before anyone tidied
them up.
