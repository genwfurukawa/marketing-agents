# Extracting the Voice

**Pulled out of your own best existing work. Never invented.**

A voice guide written from scratch describes how a company wishes it sounded. An
extracted one describes how it actually sounds when it is writing well, which is the
only version an agent can reproduce and a reader will recognise.

## The method

**1. Collect the good stuff.** Ten to fifteen pieces the team is genuinely proud of.
Posts, emails, docs, call transcripts. Include at least two things that were not
written for publication, because that is where the real register shows up.

**2. Collect one piece of the bad stuff.** Something on brand but lifeless. You need
the contrast to describe what is missing.

**3. Find what repeats.** Sentence length and variation. Where they start a paragraph.
Whether they use questions. How they handle a claim they cannot fully support. What
they do instead of a joke.

**4. Write the rules as tests, not adjectives.** "Confident and approachable" cannot
be checked. These can:

- Sentences vary between 4 and 30 words. Three medium sentences in a row is a flag.
- Claims carry a source in the same sentence, or they get cut.
- The subject is the reader or their business, never the tool.
- No em dashes. Commas, periods and colons only.
- Banned: seamless, powerful, cutting-edge, unlock, supercharge, leverage,
  AI-powered, revolutionize.

**5. Keep the receipts.** Every rule cites the piece it came from. When someone
disputes a rule later, and they will, the source settles it.

## Uneven is the point

Real writing has uneven paragraph lengths. Some are one line, some are four.
Smoothing that out is the single fastest way to make human copy read as generated,
so write the rule down before an agent optimises it away.

## Output

`brain/BRAND.md`, which `gates/` checks every draft against. A voice guide nothing
enforces is a document, not a gate.
